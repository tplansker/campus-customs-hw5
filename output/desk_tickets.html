Campus Customs: Desk Tickets

The plan for each open ticket (written before the run) next to what the agents actually did. Shop date 2026-08-31. Data: data/campus_customs_new.db

Expected workflow (every ticket)

  1. Triage and delegation: the Boss assesses the ticket and delegates it to the right specialist (Inventory, Accounting, Facilities or Customer Service) through their connected agent loops.
  2. Data retrieval: the assigned specialist uses the MCP tools to pull the relevant facts from data/campus_customs_new.db.
  3. Cross-functional collaboration: because the agents are fully connected, they delegate to each other when a ticket spans departments (for example, Customer Service asking Inventory for a stock check).
  4. Factual resolution: the team builds a resolution entirely from database records and never invents data.

One fact links all three tickets: cash is $3,400. Invoice 501 ($840, 3 days overdue) and rent ($2,400) together leave $160, and Bulldog Print Co won't ship anything until invoice 501 is paid. Every payment waits for a human.

Every answer below is about this app and the three tickets the team ran in Problem 9. The Evidence links open the ticket and Cash tabs on this page.

1. How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket and why?

Overall the team reached the right result on all three tickets. Every number came from the database, no money moved without approval, and the shop's rules held. Checking went from $3,400 to $152, and that matches cash_accounts. How efficiently it got there, and how good its judgment was, varied a lot by ticket.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

#102 for sure, and honestly most of #101 too. Rent is a straight line: look up the lease, check cash, request the payment. Splitting that across Boss → Facilities → Accounting added handoffs and tokens without improving the decision. #102 used 4 handoffs and 65,592 tokens to produce a single $2,400 approval. One agent with get_lease, get_cash_position and request_payment (all already on the MCP server) would have done it in a single loop.

#101 is also a fixed sequence: check stock, see that the vendor is blocked by invoice 501, request the payment, then request the PO. In the run it spread across 14 handoffs and 186,908 tokens, and Customer Service wrote 3 drafts of the same update. One agent with check_stock, plan_restock, request_payment, request_purchase_order and draft_customer_message could follow it directly. #103 is the one ticket where splitting the work was worth it, because stock, pricing and the customer message are three real judgments. Its weak point was the missing handoff between Inventory and Accounting, not the number of agents.

  1. Back-to-school restock planning. "Which SKUs are low before move-in week, and what can we afford to reorder?" Inventory uses list_inventory, check_shortfall and plan_restock. Accounting checks get_cash_position, ranks the reorders by margin with calculate_margin, and calls request_purchase_order only for vendors with can_ship = true. Why it would work here: it's the same chain the team already ran. plan_restock priced #103's 12-hoodie shortfall at $264, and request_purchase_order placed #101's PO once can_ship turned true.
    #103 → Inventory tools Cash step 2: PO #1

Accounting uses list_invoices (with the overdue and days_overdue fields) and get_cash_position to decide which bill to pay first and which vendor to unblock. The team flagged it as 3 days overdue, got it paid before ordering, and then paid the rent from what was left. The Cash tab shows that order: $840 first, then $2,400, leaving $152.

5. Describe three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could not solve with the tools you built, and explain the tools and agents you would need to solve them

  1. Running out of cash with no way to earn it. We left revenue out of the model, so cash only goes down, and after these tickets checking is at $152. The team can't record a sale, take a deposit for the hoodie order, or forecast cash flow. #103 shows the cost: the approved quote for 8 hoodies ($293.36) brings in nothing, and the 12-hoodie restock ($264) couldn't be bought. We'd need:
    • Tools: record_sale, record_deposit and get_sales_history.
    • Database: the SQLite trigger that blocks balance increases would have to change to allow deposits.
    • A new agent: a Sales/Cashier agent that rings up orders. Accounting could then build a cash forecast before approving POs.
    Cash: End $152.00, step 4
  2. Actually talking to customers and vendors. There's no send tool and no inbox, so drafts just sit on the board: draft #3 to Tauhid and draft #6 to the Yale AI Club were never sent. The team also can't read replies, like the Yale AI Club agreeing to buy 8 at the approved $36.67. And it can't negotiate with Bulldog, for example asking for a payment plan or a partial payment on the $840 invoice. We'd need:
    • Tools: read_inbox, a send_message that only sends after a human approves it, and a partial-payment option in request_payment.
    • A new agent: a Communications agent that turns incoming email into tickets and follow-ups.
    #101 → draft #3 #103 → draft #6 Cash step 1: invoice 501
  3. Physical maintenance problems, like a leaking roof or a broken heat press at the Chapel Street shop. Facilities can only read leases: on #102 its whole toolset was get_lease, list_leases and add_ticket_note. It has no record of maintenance contractors, work orders or equipment. We'd need:
    • Tools: create_work_order, list_service_vendors and get_equipment_status.
    • Facilities: a cost-approval path to Accounting. It already uses one for rent: the Facilities → Accounting handoff on #102.
    • Possibly a rule: that a landlord-responsibility issue goes to the landlord and isn't paid by the shop. That would need the lease terms stored in a structured way rather than as free-text notes.
    #102 → Facilities tools and handoff
